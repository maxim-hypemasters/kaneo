title: "Calendar and Gantt"
description: "See when work is due and how scheduled tasks fit together."
---

Open a project, choose **Tasks**, then open **Display** and pick **Calendar** or **Gantt** as the layout. Both views use the dates on your tasks, and your search and filters apply to them too. **Display** also sets the first day of the week.

## Calendar

Use Calendar to review work by month. Use the arrows to change months and **Today** to return to the current month. Click a task to open its details. On busy days, open the overflow indicator to see the remaining tasks.

## Gantt

Use Gantt when you want to see scheduled tasks across time, such as the work leading up to a launch.

The timeline scrolls continuously: turn the mouse wheel over the timeline (over the task list it scrolls the rows), swipe sideways on a trackpad, or drag an empty part of the timeline. It keeps extending in both directions as you go. The date field shows the first day in view; pick a date to jump there, use the arrows to move about a screen at a time, or choose **Jump to today**.

A task needs at least a start date or a due date to appear in either view. With only one date it occupies a single day; with both it spans the range. Scheduled backlog tasks are included, while archived tasks are excluded.

Set a start date and a due date on a task to give its schedule a clear span. Open the task when you need to update its owner, scope, or dates.

Dates and status answer different questions. Reaching a due date does not finish a task; move it to a done column when the work is complete.

For unscheduled ideas, use the [backlog](/core/functional/backlog-planning). For daily status changes, use [Board or List](/core/functional/plan-and-execute-tasks).

### Milestones

Milestones replace Feature scheduling. Each milestone uses its own lead and explicit start/end dates and shows its owning Feature beside the title, so duplicate names remain distinguishable. Click its bar or open action to edit it in the milestone panel. Dragging or resizing changes only that milestone; linked task dates stay unchanged. Features remain knowledge links and do not have scheduled bars.

### Dependencies

When one task blocks another (set under **Relations** in the task), Gantt draws an arrow from the end of the blocking task to the start of the task it blocks. A blocked task may start on the day its blocker ends, but not before; an arrow turns red and dashed when that's no longer true.

If dragging or resizing a bar would break a dependency, Kaneo asks first and lists the tasks that would move. **Move N related tasks** keeps everything in order: tasks it blocks are pushed later and tasks blocking it are pulled earlier, along the whole chain, each keeping its length. Overlaps that were already there stay as they are. **Move only this task** keeps just your change, and **Cancel** puts the bar back. When the tasks block each other in a loop and moving them would come back around to the task you moved, only **Move only this task** and **Cancel** are offered.

### Releases

If the project has [releases](/core/functional/releases), Gantt marks each release's dates with lines that run down the whole chart, behind the bars:

| Date | Line | Icon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Dotted | Flag |
| Code freeze | Dashed | Snowflake |
| Submission | Solid | Paper plane |

The line styles differ, so you can tell the dates apart without relying on color. When dates of several releases fall on the same day, their lines sit side by side.

A **Releases** strip under the days at the top names the release at each line. Select a label to open that release, or hover it to see the release, the date, and which date it is. Where dates are too close together for names, the labels show just their icons.

The lines follow the **Release** filter. Filter by a release to see only its tasks and its lines; with **is not**, the other releases' lines stay. Filtering for **No release** alone hides every line.

### Group by assignee / lead

Choose **Assignee / Lead** under **Display → Group by** for a lane per member, plus **Unassigned**. Tasks/Bugs use their assignees; milestones use their leads. Dragging a milestone to another member's lane changes its lead when you have assignment permission. Task reassignment works the same way. Moving sideways may also edit that item's dates. **Esc** cancels a drag. On touch screens, use the details panel.

Milestone bars sit on the lead's header row, with Tasks and Bugs underneath. Milestones with overlapping dates use separate tracks in that header. Collapsing a lead hides the task rows while keeping the milestones visible.

### Group by milestone

Choose **Milestone** for one lane per milestone and **No milestone** for unassigned work. Milestones without dates keep a lane without a bar. Each lane identifies its owning Feature. The saved older **Feature** grouping preference becomes **Milestone**.

Task filters apply to Tasks and Bugs; the separate entity controls show or hide milestones, Tasks and Bugs. Milestone planning remains visible independently of task-only filters. Calculated progress and manual status remain separate.

Dragging a Task/Bug into a milestone lane assigns it only when that milestone belongs to the task's existing Feature. Dropping into **No milestone** clears membership. A drag never changes the Feature implicitly. Milestone moves do not shift child dates. Dependencies remain between Tasks/Bugs; Feature relations do not produce milestone dependency arrows.
