title: "Calendar and Gantt"
description: "See when work is due and how scheduled tasks fit together."
---

Open a project, choose **Tasks**, then open **Display** and pick **Calendar** or **Gantt** as the layout. Both views use the dates on your tasks, and your search and filters apply to them too. **Display** also sets the first day of the week.

## Calendar

Use Calendar to review work by month. Use the arrows to change months and **Today** to return to the current month. Click a task to open its details. On busy days, open the overflow indicator to see the remaining tasks.

## Gantt

Use Gantt when you want to see scheduled tasks across time, such as the work leading up to a launch.

The timeline scrolls continuously: turn the mouse wheel over the timeline (over the task list it scrolls the rows), swipe sideways on a trackpad, or drag an empty part of the timeline. It keeps extending in both directions as you go. The date field shows the first day in view; pick a date to jump there, use the arrows to move about a screen at a time, or choose **Jump to today**.

A task needs at least a start date or a due date to appear in either view. With only one date it occupies a single day; with both it spans the range. Scheduled backlog tasks are included, while archived tasks are excluded.

Set a start date and a due date on a task to give its schedule a clear span. Open the task when you need to update its owner, scope, or dates.

Dates and status answer different questions. Reaching a due date does not finish a task; move it to a done column when the work is complete.

For unscheduled ideas, use the [backlog](/core/functional/backlog-planning). For daily status changes, use [Board or List](/core/functional/plan-and-execute-tasks).

### Features

[Features](/core/functional/plan-and-execute-tasks#group-work-into-features) look different from tasks. A task is an outlined, lightly tinted bar; a feature is a solid summary bar with small brackets under its ends. It shows the features icon (stacked layers), the feature's name, and, once it has tasks, how many of them are done, such as **3/5**. In the task list, a feature's row has the same icon and shows the count after its dates.

Without grouping, each feature is a row like any other task. Drag or resize its bar to change the feature's own dates, and click it to open the feature. Its tasks keep their dates.

### Dependencies

When one task blocks another (set under **Relations** in the task), Gantt draws an arrow from the end of the blocking task to the start of the task it blocks. A blocked task may start on the day its blocker ends, but not before; an arrow turns red and dashed when that's no longer true.

If dragging or resizing a bar would break a dependency, Kaneo asks first and lists the tasks that would move. **Move N related tasks** keeps everything in order: tasks it blocks are pushed later and tasks blocking it are pulled earlier, along the whole chain, each keeping its length. Overlaps that were already there stay as they are. **Move only this task** keeps just your change, and **Cancel** puts the bar back. When the tasks block each other in a loop and moving them would come back around to the task you moved, only **Move only this task** and **Cancel** are offered.

### Group by assignee

To see who is carrying which work, open **Display** and set **Group by** to **Assignee**. The timeline then shows one lane per project member, plus **Unassigned**, and your choice is remembered for next time. Each lane header shows the member's task count and a thin line spanning their earliest start to their latest due date. Click a header to collapse or expand its lane. If you filter by assignee, only the matching lanes are shown.

If you can assign tasks, drag a task bar into another member's lane to reassign it, or into **Unassigned** to clear its assignee. Scroll until the whole bar is on screen first. Moving the bar sideways at the same time also changes its dates. Press **Esc** to cancel a drag. On touch screens, open the task and change its assignee there instead.

Grouped by assignee, features leave the task rows and appear on the lines of the members doing their tasks as summary bars with the features icon and the feature's name, each spanning that member's own tasks in the feature. Click a feature's bar to open it. Overlapping features stack up to three high. Each group of overlapping features that doesn't fit gets a **+N** chip that lists them, and the chip stays on screen while you scroll across their dates. A feature with none of its tasks on the chart keeps its own row. A feature that blocks or is blocked by another task keeps its row as well as its bars, so its dependency arrows and warnings still show.

### Group by feature

To follow each feature's work, open **Display** and set **Group by** to **Feature**. The timeline then shows one lane per feature with its tasks under it, and a **No feature** lane last for tasks that aren't in one. Lanes are ordered by when their work starts; features with nothing on the chart follow, by name.

Each lane header shows the feature's own bar and, under it, a thin line spanning its tasks' earliest start to their latest due date, so you can see whether the feature's dates still cover its work. Each task's bar starts with its assignee's avatar, so you can see who is doing what. A bar too short for the avatar leaves it out, and the task list still names the assignee. Drag or resize the feature's bar to change its own dates. Click the bar, or the open button next to the feature's name, to open the feature. Click the name to collapse or expand the lane's tasks; the feature's bar stays in view.

Your search and filters still apply. Every feature that matches them gets a lane, even one with no dated tasks yet, so you can drag tasks into it. A feature without dates has no bar in its header until it gets a start or due date. A feature that's filtered out keeps its lane, without its own bar, while any of its tasks are on the chart.

If you can edit tasks, drag a task bar into another feature's lane to move it into that feature, or into **No feature** to take it out of its feature. Its assignee doesn't change. As with assignee lanes, scroll until the whole bar is on screen first. Moving the bar sideways at the same time also changes its dates, and **Esc** cancels the drag. On touch screens, open the task and change its feature there instead.
