title: "Calendar and Gantt"
description: "See when work is due and how scheduled tasks fit together."
---

Open a project, choose **Tasks**, then open **Display** and pick **Calendar** or **Gantt** as the layout. Both views show the published schedule. Search and filters change what you see; they never change the inputs used to plan the project. **Display** also sets the first day of the week and, for Gantt, can turn on **Hide weekends**.

## Estimate and plan work

Enter an **Estimate** on each Task or Bug, including work composed inside Features and milestones. Use short durations such as `4h`, `1d`, `3w`, or `1d 4h`. One day means eight hours of effort, and one week means five working days (40 hours). Fractional values are accepted when the resulting effort is a whole number of minutes. Saved effort is independent of the displayed shorthand.

Every assignee uses Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00 in the project's planning timezone. No member calendar setup is required. Approved [time off](/core/functional/time-off) and the public holidays of the assignee's [holiday calendar](/core/functional/time-off#public-holidays) remove their days off, as whole days in the planning timezone; pending and declined requests change nothing. The first release plan uses the manager's browser timezone if the project does not already have a planning timezone. Other projects' workloads remain independent.

Assign work to a release, open its detail page, and choose **Plan release** beside **New**. A manager with project-update permission and update permission on tasks, bugs and milestones can review the complete release, regardless of filters. Edit assignees, estimates, and priorities in the input dialog, then choose **Preview** to calculate and open its proposed Gantt timeline. The planner uses today's full working hours in the project's planning timezone or begins on the release's **Start** date if it comes later. The read-only preview groups cards by proposed assignee, with each card spanning its scheduled date columns. The conflict list highlights the finish date of work that misses code freeze. Choose **Apply plan** there to publish the reviewed release atomically. Closing either popup publishes nothing and does not undo task properties already saved.

The planner includes assigned, estimated ready work and subtracts logged time across all contributors. Running timers are measured at the preview's timestamp. In Progress work and prerequisites come first, followed by priority and stable manual order. Each assignee works on one item at a time, skipping nights, weekends, their approved time off, and their public holidays. Other releases' published placements reserve capacity wherever they overlap the planning window. Lower-priority work fills gaps only when the whole item fits without delaying reserved work. Backlog, missing estimates or assignees, exhausted estimates, and unresolved dependencies appear in planning feedback. Increase an exhausted total estimate before unfinished work or its dependents can be scheduled. Planning is bounded to ten years of availability.

Changes to estimates, assignees, priorities, workflow, logged time, dependencies, release dates, an assignee's approved time off, and their holiday calendar mark plans outdated. Time off approved, changed, or cancelled after it ended, such as a sick day recorded the following week, doesn't. The published placement stays visible until another plan is applied. Changes during review invalidate the preview; recalculate before applying. Preview tokens expire after 15 minutes.

Start and finish dates change only when a plan is applied or when you [move work in Gantt](#move-work). Existing manual dates remain visible and are preserved in history. Applying a release replaces its unfinished placements, clearing dates for items that cannot be scheduled; other releases and work without a release keep their current placement. Completed placements remain as history. Applied finish times drive overdue indicators and reminders. The preview stays open after applying and shows **Applied**; the main Gantt also refreshes. An expired proposal or changed planning input marks the retained preview outdated and disables Apply; choose **Refresh preview** to review a new proposal.

## Calendar

Use Calendar to review work by month. Use the arrows to change months and **Today** to return to the current month. Click a task to open its details. On busy days, open the overflow indicator to see the remaining tasks.

## Gantt

Use Gantt when you want to see scheduled tasks across time, such as the work leading up to a launch.

The chart scrolls the way your wheel or trackpad moves: a mouse wheel scrolls the rows, and a sideways swipe, a tilt wheel or a Magic Mouse scrolls the timeline, both at once if you move diagonally. You can also drag an empty part of the timeline. It keeps extending in both directions as you go. The date field shows the first day in view; pick a date to jump there, use the arrows to move about a screen at a time, or choose **Jump to today**.

Hold **Shift** while scrolling over the timeline to zoom: scroll up to make days wider, or down to see more days. Zoom stays anchored to the date beneath the pointer. Every day keeps its label, and row heights and text sizes stay the same. Zoom is bounded so daily labels remain readable. Your scale stays in place while filtering, grouping, resizing the window, or receiving live updates; opening another project or starting a new visit restores the default scale. **Ctrl/Command** wheel zoom remains controlled by the browser.

A Task/Bug appears once it has a published placement. Before the first plan is applied, older single-date work appears as one day and older two-date work spans its range. Archived work is excluded.

Task/Bug cards fill each day they have work on and nearly the full height of their row, so a two-hour task is as easy to read and grab as a full day. Days without work, such as a weekend or time off, show as gaps. Work done back to back on one day shares that day's column; the task rail shows the exact start and finish, and hovering or keyboard-focusing a bar reads out its exact working times.

### Move work

Drag a Task/Bug bar sideways to move it; this needs update permission for that kind of work. Planned work keeps its planned assignee and remaining effort and is laid out again through working hours (Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00 in the planning timezone) from the day you drop it on, at the same time of day, skipping its planned assignee's approved time off and public holidays as Apply does. Work dropped on a weekend or on one of those days starts the next working morning. Work without a plan keeps its length. A moved item stays where you put it, and other releases' plans work around it, until the next Apply of its own release plans it again. Bars can also be resized: drag the handle at either end, or focus the handle and press the left or right arrow key, to change the start or due date. A resized bar takes exactly the dates you give it; planned work then stops following its estimate until the next Apply of its release. Work without a plan, and resized bars, can't start or end on a day the assignee is away, though they may span such days; pending requests never block. While you drag, a hint names who is away and on which day, and dropping there puts the bar back and says why. An arrow key steps past those days to the next day the date can take. See [Time off and public holidays](#time-off-and-public-holidays). **Esc** cancels a drag. On touch screens a drag scrolls the timeline instead.

Rows stay where they are while bars are moved or resized, by you or by others, so the chart doesn't reshuffle under you; reopen the Gantt or reload the page to sort them by start date again. With **Compact rows**, a bar that no longer fits beside its neighbours moves to the first row with room, and the other bars stay put.

**Hide weekends** in **Display** removes the Saturday and Sunday columns from the Gantt. It's off until you turn it on, and the choice is saved in your browser and only affects your view. Dates don't change: a bar across a weekend is drawn without the gap, and one whose dates are all on a weekend is drawn one day wide on the following Monday. Dragging moves a bar by working days and keeps its working-day length, resizing and **Move N related tasks** never put a start or due date on a hidden weekend day, and today and release dates that fall on a weekend are marked between Friday and Monday. The Calendar and the release plan preview still show every day.

Dates and status answer different questions. Reaching a due date does not finish a task; move it to a done column when the work is complete.

For unscheduled ideas, use the [backlog](/core/functional/backlog-planning). For daily status changes, use [Board or List](/core/functional/plan-and-execute-tasks).

### Milestones

Milestones replace Feature scheduling. Each milestone uses its own lead and a read-only range derived from its nonarchived linked Tasks and Bugs and shows its owning Feature beside the title, so duplicate names remain distinguishable. Click its bar or open action to edit it in the milestone panel. Applying a release plan updates milestone bounds across all linked work and releases. Features remain knowledge links and do not have scheduled bars.

### Dependencies

When one Task/Bug blocks another (set under **Relations**), Gantt connects the blocker's final segment to its successor's first segment. The successor may begin at the same instant its blocker finishes. Prerequisites inherit the urgency of work they unblock. Completed blockers are satisfied; cycles and unschedulable blockers leave affected work unscheduled. Related relations do not create execution dependencies, and Features have no relations.

If moving a bar would break a dependency, Kaneo asks first and lists the work that would move. **Move N related tasks** keeps everything in order: work it blocks is pushed later and work blocking it is pulled earlier, along the whole chain, by whole days. Overlaps that were already there stay as they are, and finished work never moves. **Move only this task** keeps just your change, and **Cancel** puts the bar back. If any task that would move can't start or end on its new dates because its assignee is away, nothing moves and Kaneo says why. When work blocks itself in a loop that moving would come back around to, only **Move only this task** and **Cancel** are offered.

An accessible blocker in another project uses its applied finish. Without an accessible applied schedule, the dependent item stays unscheduled with generic feedback. Private blocker titles and dates are not exposed.

### Releases

If the project has [releases](/core/functional/releases), Gantt marks each release's dates with lines that run down the whole chart, behind the bars:

| Date | Line | Icon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Dotted | Flag |
| Code freeze | Dashed | Snowflake |
| Submission | Solid | Paper plane |

The line styles differ, so you can tell the dates apart without relying on color. When dates of several releases fall on the same day, their lines sit side by side.

Release Start is an earliest planning boundary. Finishing after Code freeze or Submission produces a warning, rather than hiding work that cannot fit. These calendar-day boundaries use the saved project planning timezone.

A **Releases** strip under the days at the top names the release at each line. Select a label to open that release, or hover it to see the release, the date, and which date it is. Where dates are too close together for names, the labels show just their icons.

The lines follow the **Release** filter. Filter by a release to see only its tasks and its lines; with **is not**, the other releases' lines stay. Filtering for **No release** alone hides every line.

### Time off and public holidays

If your role includes **View time off**, Gantt shows when assignees are away: approved [time off](/core/functional/time-off) and the public holidays of their [holiday calendar](/core/functional/time-off#public-holidays). Pending requests never appear.

- Grouped by **Assignee / Lead**, each member's lane has a band across the days they are away.
- In every grouping, a Task/Bug bar has a thin strip under the days it covers while its assignee is away. The strip follows the bar while you drag it.
- Bands show the type of time off, or the holiday's name, for your own days away and, if you can approve time off, everyone's. Otherwise they read **Away**.

Planned work is laid out around those days. Work without a plan and resized bars can't start or end on one, though they may span them. Kaneo checks only the dates a change moves, or both when the work goes to someone else, so approving time off over existing work is allowed: the strip shows the conflict, and the work keeps its dates until someone changes them.

The same rule applies outside Gantt:

- A task's **Start** and **Due** date pickers, and the due dates in a card's right-click menu, strike through the assignee's days away. Those days can't be picked, and quick dates that land on one aren't offered.
- Setting a due date for several tasks from the bulk bar fails as a whole when any of them would be due on a day its assignee is away. The message names the tasks, and nothing is saved.
- Assigning work with dates set by hand to someone away on its start or due day fails and says why.

Seeing days away needs **View time off**, but the rule always applies: without that permission, a change that breaks it still fails, and the message names only the date. Dates are read as days in your browser's timezone. If time off can't be loaded, Gantt says so above the chart with a button to try again. See [Task dates](/core/functional/time-off#task-dates) for the API and MCP.

### Group by assignee / lead

Choose **Assignee / Lead** under **Display → Group by** for a lane per member, plus **Unassigned**. Published Task/Bug placements retain their planned assignee until another Apply; milestones use their current leads. Reassigning a Task/Bug changes the planning input and marks the schedule outdated. Dragging a milestone to another member's lane changes its lead when you have assignment permission. Dragging a Task/Bug into another member's lane reassigns it, and dragging it sideways at the same time also moves it, in one step: both changes save, or neither does. Work without a plan can't go to a member who is away on its start or due day: their lane isn't highlighted while you drag, and the hint says who is away. **Esc** cancels a drag. On touch screens, use the details panel.

Milestone bars sit on the lead's header row, with Tasks and Bugs underneath. Milestones with overlapping dates use separate tracks in that header. Collapsing a lead hides the task rows while keeping the milestones visible.

### Group by milestone

Choose **Milestone** for one lane per milestone and **No milestone** for unassigned work. Milestones without dates keep a lane without a bar. Each lane identifies its owning Feature. The saved older **Feature** grouping preference becomes **Milestone**.

Task filters apply to Tasks and Bugs; the separate entity controls show or hide milestones, Tasks and Bugs. Milestone planning remains visible independently of task-only filters. Milestone status is read-only and derived from all nonarchived linked Tasks/Bugs; task filters do not change it. Progress keeps its existing counting rules.

Dragging a Task/Bug into a milestone lane assigns it only when that milestone belongs to the task's existing Feature. Dropping into **No milestone** clears membership. A drag never changes the Feature implicitly. Milestone lead changes do not shift child dates. Their date ranges use all nonarchived linked work across releases, regardless of task filters. Dependencies remain between Tasks/Bugs; Features have no relations, so milestones get no dependency arrows from them.
